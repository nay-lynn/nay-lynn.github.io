About Me

Hi! I'm Naylynn. I currently work at the SETI Institute as the Technical Project Manager for NASA’s Pandora mission.

I graduated from Smith College in 2023 with a degree in Statistical & Data Sciences. As an undergraduate, I conducted research at several institutions, including NASA, MIT, Caltech, Stanford, and the Harvard-Smithsonian Center for Astrophysics.

Originally from Puerto Rico, I’m passionate about the intersection of culture and science, expanding educational accessibility, and bringing more diversity of thought into the scientific process. I love mentoring students, supporting inclusive outreach efforts, and helping others see themselves in STEM.

When I’m not working or thinking about distant planets, you can usually find me roller skating, reading, playing video games, hanging out with my pup, or out in nature - bird watching, cataloging plants and flowers, or collecting rocks!

Outreach

Teaching
I co-created a classroom curriculum that explored the intersection of science and art, focusing on scientific research and artistic traditions from Puerto Rico. Designed for K–12 students, the program used hands-on activities, storytelling, and creative expression to make STEM more engaging and culturally meaningful—especially for students from under-resourced communities.

Mentorship
As a member of the BRIEF Astronomy Program, I mentored student research groups throughout every stage of the scientific process—from developing their initial research proposals to collecting data, analyzing results, and preparing their findings for both presentations and publication. I worked closely with students to help them refine their ideas, navigate challenges in data analysis, and gain confidence in communicating their work.

Public Engagement
While working with the Harvard–Smithsonian Center for Astrophysics, I led outreach efforts for the AIR-Spec instrument team during the 2019 total solar eclipse in Perú. This included delivering an hour-long talk in Spanish at the Universidad Nacional de Ingeniería and engaging with local media, embassy officials, and scientists who visited our airborne observatory. Our work was later featured on the front page of a major newspaper in Lima, the capital of Perú.

STEM Access & Inclusion
While attending San Diego Mesa College, I served as chapter president for SACNAS (Society for Advancement of Chicanos/Hispanics and Native Americans in Science). Together with a group of peers, we created a supportive community focused on academic success, regularly hosting study groups and networking events. I also organized workshops on applying to research programs, writing personal statements, and navigating the path to graduate school.
